\resultbox{When only temporal pairing changes, the learned predictor changes

\section{Question}

We seek a representation $z_t=f_\theta(x_t)$ and a linear predictor $M$ such
that
\[
M z_t\approx z_{t+1}.
\]
If $\psi(r_t)$ describes the true phase, $K$ is its operator, and the encoder
learns $z\approx A\psi$, the relevant test is
\[
\boxed{MA\approx AK}.
\]
The encoder may choose any latent basis, so we do not require $M=K$. We compare
both the action of $M$ and its spectrum restricted to the active latent span.

\paragraph{Theoretical motivation.}
Ruiz-Morales et al. show that, under idealized assumptions, a time-series JEPA
can encode dynamical-regime indicators: Koopman eigenfunctions with
$\lambda=1$. A near-identity linear predictor selects this interpretable
invariant solution from equivalent optima. This experiment asks the natural
next question: can the same predictive architecture move beyond invariants and
recover nontrivial modes such as $\{-1,i,-i\}$ and
$\rho\{-1,i,-i\}$? See
\href{https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i30.39708}{Ruiz-Morales et al.,
``Koopman Invariants as Drivers of Emergent Time-Series Clustering in
Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures,'' AAAI 2026}.

\section{Controlled observations}

The hidden state has four phases $r\in\{0,1,2,3\}$. Each phase emits a
length-128 window:
\[
x_t[\tau]=a_t\,s_{r_t}(\tau-\delta_t)+b_t+\epsilon_{t,\tau},
\]
with random amplitude, offset, temporal jitter, and noise. Every condition
reuses exactly the same current and future window banks; only temporal pairing
changes.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Dynamics} & \textbf{Transition} & \textbf{Active spectrum}\\
\midrule
Static & $r_{t+1}=r_t$ & $\{1,1,1\}$\\
Cyclic & $r_{t+1}=r_t+1\pmod 4$ & $\{-1,i,-i\}$\\
Independent & uniform future phase & $\{0,0,0\}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Each seed and condition contains 1,024 training pairs and 256 validation pairs.
The main comparison uses 10 paired seeds. The observation audit verifies
identical single-window marginals, correct transitions, and perfectly
decodable phase.

Training runs for 60 epochs with AdamW. The predictor learning rate is
$4\times$ the encoder rate; the online encoder is frozen after epoch 3 and the
target follows an EMA update. Mean, variance, and covariance penalties prevent
collapse.

The 3D latent matches the true dynamic rank, and the early encoder freeze
stabilizes predictor optimization. Both are part of the experimental scope.

\section{The predictor follows the transition law}

For each candidate $d$, we compute
\[
E_d=\frac{\lVert MA-AK_d\rVert_F}{\lVert A\rVert_F}.
\]
We also compare the eigenvalues of $M$ on the active span. The predeclared
criterion is at least 8/10 correct seeds by action and spectrum. Training loss
does not enter this decision.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Dynamics} & \textbf{Action} & \textbf{Spectrum} & \textbf{Error} & \textbf{Margin}\\
\midrule
Static & 10/10 & 10/10 & 0.142 & 0.806\\
Cyclic & 10/10 & 10/10 & 0.068 & 0.877\\
Independent & 10/10 & 10/10 & 0.015 & 0.981\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The learned cyclic action contains the nontrivial active spectrum
$\{-1,i,-i\}$. Identification is strongest at one step. Repeated application
of $M$ magnifies small discrepancies, so the experiment does not claim perfect
long-horizon prediction.

\section{Continuous dynamic persistence}

We interpolate between the cyclic law $C$ and a uniform law $U$:
\[
P_\rho=\rho C+(1-\rho)U,
\qquad K_\rho=\rho C,
\qquad \sigma(K_\rho)=\rho\{-1,i,-i\}.
\]

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
\toprule
$\boldsymbol{\rho}$ & \textbf{Action} & \textbf{Spectrum} & \textbf{Median modulus}\\
\midrule
0.00 & 10/10 & 10/10 & 0.016\\
0.25 & 10/10 & 10/10 & 0.239\\
0.50 & 9/10 & 9/10 & 0.476\\
0.75 & 8/10 & 8/10 & 0.718\\
1.00 & 7/10 & 7/10 & 0.948\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/decay_spectral_calibration.png}
\caption{Left: learned spectral modulus against persistence. Right: expected

Continuous calibration has MAE 0.027, slope 0.937, intercept 0.011, and
$R^2=0.9998$. The predictor is slightly contractive at high persistence. Some
seeds therefore choose the neighboring grid value, and the strict 8/10
criterion does not pass at $\rho=1$. This is an observed behavior, not replaced
by a post-hoc threshold.

\section{Conclusion}

\textbf{What the experiment establishes:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item JEPA does not collapse to an invariant representation.
  \item The predictor changes when only temporal pairing changes.
  \item Its active action and spectrum recover three qualitatively different laws.
  \item Its spectral radius tracks continuous persistence.
  \item Small one-step errors accumulate in multi-step rollouts.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Scope:} the result depends on this observation process, 3D latent,
early encoder freeze, EMA, and anti-collapse regularization. It does not imply
universal Koopman recovery or robustness to different observations and state
spaces. It is a controlled characterization of this JEPA's learned dynamics.
